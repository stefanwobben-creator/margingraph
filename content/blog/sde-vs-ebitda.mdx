Two figures, calculated from the same accounts, that can differ by a hundred thousand euros. The mistake is never in the arithmetic. It is in applying a multiple built for one to the other.

## What each one includes

    ["Answers", "What one owner-operator takes home", "What the business earns independently"],

/>

The last row is the whole distinction. SDE describes a job plus a business. EBITDA describes a business.

## The same company, both ways

<Metrics
  items={[
    { label: "Operating profit", value: "€185,000", note: "as reported" },
    { label: "SDE", value: "€312,000", note: "+ owner €95k, + car €14k, + one-offs €18k" },
    { label: "EBITDA", value: "€247,000", note: "same add-backs, then − €65k market-rate manager" },
  ]}
/>

The €65,000 gap is what it would cost to hire someone to do what the owner does. SDE assumes the buyer does that work themselves. EBITDA assumes they pay someone.

## Why the multiples are different, and not by a little

<Comparison
  columns={["", "Typical multiple", "Implied price on the figures above"]}
  rows={[
    ["SDE", "2–3.5×", "€624k – €1.09M"],
    ["EBITDA", "4–6×", "€988k – €1.48M"],
  ]}
/>

SDE multiples are lower precisely because SDE is a larger number containing a salary. They are two halves of the same equation and neither is generous.

<Warning title="The €400,000 mistake">
  Apply a 5× EBITDA multiple to an SDE figure and you get €1.56M for a business
  that will transact around €1.1M. Owners arrive at a broker with that number,
  spend nine months finding out it is not real, and often come out of the
  process worse than if they had started honestly.
</Warning>

## Which one applies to you

<Checklist
  items={[
    "One owner working full time in the business, under roughly €1M turnover → SDE",
    "The buyer will step into the owner's job personally → SDE",
    "A management layer runs day-to-day operations → EBITDA",
    "Above roughly €1M of turnover, or €500k of earnings → EBITDA",
    "Private equity or a strategic buyer is in the process → EBITDA, without exception",
  ]}
/>

If you are between the two, calculate both. The gap tells you what your own involvement is worth in the sale, which is useful information regardless of which figure you present.

## The second working owner problem

Two founders both working full time, one salary between them. SDE adds back **one** owner's compensation, not two — the second is a genuine operating cost, because the buyer will have to replace that person.

Owners routinely add back both and inflate SDE by an entire salary. It is the first thing a buyer's advisor recalculates, and it costs credibility on everything else in the file.

## What buyers do with the number

Neither figure is accepted as presented. A buyer will recalculate it from your accounts, and their version will be lower — that is not adversarial, it is the job. [What buyers look for](/blog/what-buyers-look-for) covers what they check first, and [how EBITDA affects your valuation](/blog/how-ebitda-affects-your-valuation) covers which add-backs survive.
