Yes — more than any other single factor. Identical businesses at €400,000 and €2,000,000 of EBITDA will not attract the same multiple, and the gap is routinely one to two full turns.

## The reason is who is allowed to bid

<Comparison
  columns={["Earnings", "Who buys", "Effect on price"]}
  rows={[
    ["Under €250k", "Individuals, own capital", "Few bidders, price set by negotiation"],
    ["€250k – €500k", "Individuals with bank debt", "Slightly deeper pool"],
    ["€500k – €2M", "Small funds, strategics, MBI teams", "Competition begins"],
    ["Above €2M", "Private equity with a mandate", "Competition sets the price"],
  ]}
/>

Below half a million of earnings the buyer is spending their own money on a business they will also work in. Above two million, funds with capital to deploy are competing with each other, and competition rather than negotiation determines the outcome.

## What this means practically

Growing from €400,000 to €600,000 of earnings can add more value than the €200,000 itself, because it may move you into a band with more bidders. That is one of the few cases where the arithmetic genuinely compounds in the seller's favour.

It also means a sale postponed for the right two years can be worth substantially more — and a sale postponed for the wrong two years, during which nothing structural changed, is worth exactly the same and costs you two years.
