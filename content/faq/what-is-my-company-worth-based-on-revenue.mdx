Between 0.3× and 8× annual revenue, depending almost entirely on business model. Distribution sits near the bottom, low-churn software near the top. But before using any of those figures, do one calculation.

## Divide the revenue multiple by your margin

Every revenue multiple is an earnings multiple wearing a disguise.

<Metrics
  items={[
    { label: "1× revenue at 25% margin", value: "4× EBITDA", note: "sensible" },
    { label: "1× revenue at 12% margin", value: "8.3× EBITDA", note: "rich for an SME" },
    { label: "1× revenue at 6% margin", value: "16.7× EBITDA", note: "not a real price" },
  ]}
/>

If the implied earnings multiple falls outside the normal two-to-six band, the revenue multiple is wrong for your business.

## When revenue is the right basis

When earnings are suppressed by deliberate reinvestment, when revenue is contractual enough to behave like an annuity, or when margins swing too violently for any single year to mean anything. Outside those three cases, a buyer will value your business on earnings whatever you present.
