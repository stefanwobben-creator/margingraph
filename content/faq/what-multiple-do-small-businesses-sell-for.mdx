Businesses under roughly €1M of turnover typically sell for **2 to 3.5 times SDE** — seller's discretionary earnings, which still includes the owner's compensation. Not EBITDA, and this is where most of the confusion in small business valuation lives.

## Why the SDE multiple looks low

Because SDE is a bigger number. It contains a salary that EBITDA removes.

<Metrics
  items={[
    { label: "SDE", value: "€268,000", note: "includes owner compensation" },
    { label: "At 2.75× SDE", value: "€737,000", note: "enterprise value" },
    { label: "Same business on EBITDA", value: "€203,000 × 4×", note: "€812,000 — similar answer" },
  ]}
/>

The two routes converge, which is the point. What does not converge is applying an EBITDA multiple to an SDE figure — that produces €1.34M for a business that transacts near €750,000.

## What sits at the top of the range

<Checklist
  items={[
    "The owner is genuinely replaceable within three months",
    "No customer above 15% of revenue",
    "Revenue is contracted or genuinely recurring",
    "Three years of consistent, reconciled accounts",
    "Premises and equipment that transfer without renegotiation",
  ]}
/>

Businesses with all five sit at 3.5× or occasionally above. Businesses with none sit at 1.5× and struggle to find a buyer at all.
