Sustained growth above roughly **15% a year** is worth half a turn to a full turn on the multiple. Two businesses at €800,000 of EBITDA, one flat and one compounding at 20%, regularly trade a full turn apart.

## Why it works that way

At 5× EBITDA a buyer is paying for years six onward. Growth makes those years bigger, so the same multiple buys more. That is the entire mechanism, and it explains what buyers actually reward.

## The trap

Growth funded by working capital that has not yet converted to cash raises EBITDA and lowers the bank balance simultaneously. Buyers look at both, and a business growing 25% while receivables grow 40% invites a very specific question.

## Growth without margin

Revenue growth with flat or falling margin is usually read as buying business rather than winning it.
