Yes, and this is where value most often leaks quietly. Buyers expect a **normal level of working capital** to transfer with the business. Anything below that level is deducted from what you receive, and the level is usually set as a twelve-month average.

## How the peg works

The logic is straightforward: the buyer is purchasing a business that can operate on day one. If you have collected receivables aggressively and stretched payables to lift the cash balance, you have moved money from working capital into cash — and the buyer will move it back.

## What owners get wrong

<Warning title="The most expensive quiet negotiation">
  Owners who fight hard for a quarter-turn on the multiple and accept the
  buyer's working capital definition without argument routinely give back more
  than they won.
</Warning>

## What to do

Understand your own seasonal pattern before anyone proposes a peg. A business whose working capital swings €300,000 between March and September has a legitimate argument about which average is fair, and that argument is much easier to make with twelve months of data than with an opinion.
