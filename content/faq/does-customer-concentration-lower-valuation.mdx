Yes, and more than almost anything else you can control. One customer above **25% of revenue** typically costs a full turn on the multiple. Above 35%, buyers frequently stop discounting and start proposing an earn-out instead.

## The typical effect

<Comparison
  columns={["Largest customer", "Effect", "Buyer's reasoning"]}
  rows={[
    ["Under 10%", "None", "Loss is absorbed"],
    ["10–20%", "Slight discount", "Uncomfortable but survivable"],
    ["20–35%", "0.5–1× off", "One conversation changes the business"],
    ["Above 35%", "1×+ off, or an earn-out", "Buying a relationship, not a company"],
  ]}
/>

## Why an earn-out rather than a discount

Because the buyer cannot price the risk, they transfer it. An earn-out ties part of your consideration to the customer still being there in two years — which is rational from their side and converts a price you agreed into a price you have to earn.

## What actually helps

<Checklist
  items={[
    "Grow the rest of the business — you cannot fix concentration by losing the customer",

    "Move the relationship off yourself and onto someone who stays",
    "Show the customer's own tenure — eleven years of history reads differently from two",
  ]}
/>
